Why The Basement Box Buffers And The Living Room One Does Not

By Priya Nair · August 05, 2026

Every January my brother-in-law asks me the same question, and every January I give him the same answer in a slightly worse mood, so this year I am writing it down.

The question is why a streaming box that behaves perfectly in the living room turns to mush in the basement. The honest answer is almost never the service. It is the twenty feet of concrete between the router and the device, and no amount of paying more per month fixes concrete.

Start by measuring rather than guessing. Run a speed test on the device itself, not on the phone in your pocket, and run it at the hour you actually watch. A line that reads two hundred megabits at ten in the morning can read fifteen at nine at night, because everyone on the street is home. Canada's own regulator publishes plain broadband measurement work worth reading (https://crtc.gc.ca) if you want to understand what your provider has actually promised you.

If the number is low, move the box before you change anything else. A cheap flat ethernet cable run along the skirting board has rescued more evenings than any setting I have ever changed. Wireless is fine when the router is in the same room and hopeless through two floors.

Only after the connection is honest does the software matter. Set the output resolution to match the panel instead of the highest number offered, turn off any frame interpolation the television calls "smooth motion", and let the guide finish loading before judging it. A guide that fills in slowly is usually pulling real listings data, which is a good sign rather than a bad one.

Then watch a full programme. Not a trailer, not a two minute clip. Faults that matter show up at the twenty minute mark, when a buffer that looked generous finally runs dry. If the picture holds through a whole football match it will hold through anything.

For the setup end of this, the walkthroughs on the iptv canada app pages are the ones I send people to, because they are written per device rather than as one generic page. And if you want the underlying idea rather than the instructions, Wikipedia's article on internet television (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_television) is a fair place to start.
